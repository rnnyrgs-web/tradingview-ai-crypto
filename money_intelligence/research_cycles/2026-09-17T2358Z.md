### Sep. 15 U.S. funding stress test: H.4.1 materially resolves the aggregate-scarcity question

OBSERVED FACT — Federal Reserve H.4.1 released Sep. 17 shows Wednesday Sep. 16 reserve balances at $2.921537T, down $114.971B from Sep. 9, while the Treasury General Account rose to $991.708B, up $148.003B. The Fed securities portfolio was essentially unchanged. This confirms that the tax/settlement window produced a large TGA rebuild and reserve drain, broadly matching the pre-event balance-sheet mechanism.

OBSERVED FACT — The weekly-average H.4.1 figures are less extreme because they smooth the window: average reserve balances were $3.013794T, +$22.484B week/week, while average TGA was $877.028B. Point-in-time Wednesday balances are therefore the more informative observation for the event-window drain, while weekly averages remain useful for regime context.

OBSERVED FACT — Previously verified Sep. 15 transaction SOFR was 3.64%, only +2 bp versus Sep. 14 and below the then-current 3.65% IORB. The combination of a roughly $115B Wednesday reserve decline and only a small median secured-funding response is a strong negative control against treating aggregate reserve drainage as a mechanical funding-stress trigger.

OBSERVED FACT — The Sep. 16 FOMC implementation note raised IORB to 3.90% effective Sep. 17 and retained standing repo at 4.00%, while explicitly preserving authority for Treasury-bill/<=3y purchases when appropriate to maintain ample reserves.

INFERENCE — The pre-event causal mechanism was directionally correct at the balance-sheet level (TGA rebuild -> reserve drain), but the predicted vulnerability did not bind broadly at the median repo rate. The sub-10%-of-GDP reserves+ON-RRP zone is therefore better interpreted as a conditional sensitivity regime, not a deterministic scarcity threshold. Distribution of reserves, dealer/MMF intermediation, collateral, exact settlement composition and Fed backstops matter materially.

HYPOTHESIS UPDATE — `low aggregate Fed liquidity + tax/settlement drain -> broad repo stress` is WEAKENED. A better model is `low aggregate liquidity increases elasticity of repo rates to balance-sheet shocks conditional on intermediation capacity becoming binding`. Future tests should use upper-tail SOFR/TGCR, SRP take-up, dealer inventory/MMF cash and reserve distribution rather than aggregate reserves alone.

FALSIFICATION / remaining uncertainty — This does not prove that all funding capacity is ample. Localized or upper-tail stress could exist despite a calm median. Complete the case with Sep. 15 SOFR/TGCR percentiles and standing-repo take-up before upgrading from `broad median scarcity not observed` to `no meaningful funding stress`.
