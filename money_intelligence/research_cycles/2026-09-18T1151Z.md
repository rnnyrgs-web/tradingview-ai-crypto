## Fresh cross-asset fund-flow evidence

OBSERVED FACT — Reuters reported on Sep. 18 that global equity funds had $23.21B of net outflows in the week ended Sep. 16, the largest weekly outflow in nine months. U.S. equity funds lost $31.44B for a fourth consecutive week; European funds lost $0.295B; Asian equity funds received $6.26B. Sector equity funds nevertheless received $4.49B, led by technology, financials and consumer discretionary. Commodity funds received $1.17B, concentrated in gold/precious metals, while energy funds lost $0.148B. Global bond funds received only $0.855B, with government and short-duration demand stronger while high-yield/euro-denominated funds saw redemptions. Money-market funds had a $77.42B outflow.

INFERENCE — The capital-flow regime is not a simple `risk -> cash` migration. Aggregate U.S. equity exposure is being reduced while investors selectively retain/add technology and other sector risk, and commodity-fund demand is favoring precious metals rather than energy. The money-market outflow also contradicts a simplistic claim that all de-risking is accumulating in cash. Source/destination matching is incomplete because the reported fund categories do not identify the same investors or prove that one category directly funded another.

HYPOTHESIS — In the current energy-inflation/high-yield regime, cross-sectional allocation may be more informative than broad risk-on/risk-off labels: capital can leave broad U.S. equity funds while still accumulating in AI/technology and gold. Test against sector ETF/fund flows, relative returns, earnings/capex revisions and rates over 1d/1w/1m windows.

FALSIFIER — Broad U.S. equity outflows reverse while sector concentration disappears, or technology/gold fail to show persistent relative strength despite continued dedicated inflows.

## Oil framework receives another out-of-sample confirmation, with an important cross-grade contradiction

OBSERVED FACT — Reuters Sep. 18 reported Brent near $103.17 and WTI near $101.30, a third session of declines as Saudi alternative shipments/restoration, larger product inventories across major hubs and higher Chinese fuel exports reduced immediate shortage expectations. Separately, Reuters reported Russian ESPO crude above $120/bbl with record $20-$30/bbl premiums to Brent as Chinese refiners sought alternative supply amid Middle-East disruption.

INFERENCE — The broad Brent reversal supports the `effective deliverable supply after substitution/inventories` framework, but the ESPO premium shows substitution is not frictionless: scarcity can migrate across grades/routes even while the global benchmark falls. A better causal state variable is therefore not just aggregate deliverable barrels but `deliverable supply by location/grade/refinery compatibility net of freight/insurance/logistics constraints`.

HYPOTHESIS UPDATE — For geopolitical crude shocks, benchmark persistence should be tested against a vector of regional/grade physical differentials and route capacity, not only headline production loss or aggregate inventories. Extreme cross-grade premiums can coexist with a falling benchmark when substitution relieves the marginal global barrel but raises the value of specific accessible grades.
