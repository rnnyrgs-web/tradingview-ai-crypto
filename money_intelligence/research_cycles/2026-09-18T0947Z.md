## Material evidence 1 — Sep. 15 U.S. liquidity drain occurred, but aggregate reserves did not collapse

OBSERVED FACT — Federal Reserve H.4.1 released Sep. 17 shows Wednesday Sep. 16 TGA at $991.708B, up $148.003B from Sep. 9, while depository-institution reserve balances were $2.921537T, down $114.971B. This directly confirms the anticipated tax/settlement/TGA cash drain mechanism. Fed securities held outright changed only about +$2.051B, so the balance-sheet movement was predominantly liability-side rather than QE/QT.

OBSERVED FACT — The weekly-average H.4.1 measure is less extreme: average reserves were $3.013794T, +$22.484B week/week, and average TGA $877.028B, -$6.307B. The large Wednesday point change therefore contains substantial timing/concentration effects and should not be extrapolated mechanically to the whole week.

INFERENCE — The pre-event causal mechanism was correct about direction of the point-in-time cash drain: Treasury cash accumulation can pull reserves down sharply when ON RRP is depleted. But the latest evidence still does not validate the stronger claim that this automatically creates funding stress or risk-asset selling. This strengthens the distinction between `liquidity drain` and `binding intermediation scarcity`.

HYPOTHESIS UPDATE — Treat TGA/reserve moves as an exposure/state variable. Require repo distribution tails, EFFR-IORB, SRF use and dealer/MMF evidence before promoting the state to a stress regime.

## Material evidence 2 — BOJ hike is a fresh expectation-adjusted policy control

OBSERVED FACT — On Sep. 18 the Bank of Japan raised its policy rate from 1.00% to 1.25%, the highest in 31 years, by a 7-2 vote. Contemporaneous Reuters reporting described the hike as widely expected. Two board members dissented in favor of patience.

OBSERVED FACT — Instead of appreciating on the nominal rate increase, the yen weakened sharply; Reuters reported USD/JPY up about 1.2% to 157.84, the largest daily dollar gain versus yen since December, as investors focused on the absence of explicitly hawkish forward guidance and the two dissents. Japanese equities also rose rather than selling off immediately.

INFERENCE — This is a high-information matched control supporting the existing candidate hypothesis that scheduled central-bank actions should be modeled as `realized policy/path minus priced distribution`, not `hike = currency up / equities down`. The headline 25 bp hike was not the marginal information; perceived future-path restraint was.

ALTERNATIVES — Global risk-on from lower oil, U.S. equity momentum, and pre-event positioning may have contributed to yen weakness/equity strength. Therefore this single event supports but does not prove the general rule.

NEXT TEST — Add BOJ Sep. 18 to the historical expectation-adjusted policy-surprise event set with timestamp-safe pre-event OIS/probability, 30m/1d/5d JGB, USDJPY, Nikkei, gold and global-risk responses.
