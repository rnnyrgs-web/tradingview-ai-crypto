## Highest-information result: Sep. 15 funding stress test weakened the near-term reserve-scarcity thesis

### OBSERVED FACT
- Federal Reserve H.4.1 released Sep. 17 shows the Treasury General Account at $991.708B on Sep. 16, up $148.003B from Sep. 9.
- Other deposits held by depository institutions (reserve balances) were $2.921537T on Sep. 16, down $114.971B week/week.
- Thus the anticipated tax/settlement/TGA rebuild produced a large reserve drain while the Fed securities portfolio was nearly unchanged.
- SOFR was 3.64% for Sep. 15, only 2 bp above Sep. 14, then returned to 3.62% for Sep. 16. This is not the broad secured-funding stress signature hypothesized before the event.
- The Sep. 16 FOMC raised the target range 25 bp to 3.75%-4.00%, IORB to 3.90% effective Sep. 17, and retained the standing repo rate at 4.00%.

### INFERENCE
The Sep. 15 event is a useful matched non-crisis control: even with reserves + ON RRP around the previously identified sub-10%-of-GDP sensitivity zone, a roughly $148B weekly TGA rise and roughly $115B reserve decline did not by themselves create a material repo-rate spike. The Fed research threshold is therefore better treated as a *sensitivity regime*, not a deterministic scarcity trigger. Distribution of reserves, MMF cash, dealer capacity, collateral demand and standing-facility credibility remain necessary conditioning variables.

### HYPOTHESIS UPDATE
WEAKEN: `aggregate Fed liquidity below 10% of GDP + large Treasury/tax drain -> near-term repo stress` when used without market-capacity conditioning.

REFINE to: `low aggregate Fed liquidity increases elasticity of repo rates to issuance/cash drains, but stress requires binding private intermediation/cash constraints; actual SOFR/TGCR distributions and SRF usage are confirmation variables, not optional extras.`

### FALSIFICATION / NEXT TEST
The refined hypothesis would be weakened further if quarter-end TGA/reserve pressure intensifies while SOFR/TGCR upper percentiles and SRF usage remain orderly. It would strengthen if a comparable cash/settlement shock produces broad secured-rate pressure after controlling for policy-rate changes and quarter-end balance-sheet effects.
