# Money Intelligence System — Canonical Operating Specification

## 1. Mission

Build a second, independent research system whose primary objective is to understand money as deeply and truthfully as possible: what money is, how it is created and destroyed, how purchasing power and credit move through institutions and markets, how money changes the real world, how the real world changes money, and how those interactions influence the prices and values of assets.

This system is **not the crypto signal engine**. It must not manufacture, alter, backfill, promote, or contaminate signal-engine evidence. It is a world/money/markets learning system that can generate separate investment hypotheses and decision-support ideas from causal economic understanding.

The north-star question is:

> Who is likely to become the marginal buyer or seller next, what will motivate them, with what money or credit, how much capital can they deploy, what supply will they encounter, what feedback loops can follow, over what horizon, and what evidence would invalidate the thesis?

The purpose is not to sound economically sophisticated. The purpose is to build progressively better, falsifiable, point-in-time understanding that improves future decisions about where capital is likely to gain or lose purchasing power.

## 2. Hard separation from the signal system

Money Intelligence may study the same assets and public markets, but it is a separate lane.

It MUST NOT:
- write to production signal tables;
- alter signal confidence, calibration, promotion gates, paper trades, broker state, or prediction history;
- reinterpret a signal result after the fact to make Money Intelligence look correct;
- use future information in historical research;
- call research, backtests, narratives, or simulated returns real profit;
- authorize or execute real-money trades.

It MAY publish separate research-backed `BUY / HOLD / REDUCE / AVOID / SHORT-THESIS / CASH` style decision-support views, but they must be clearly labeled as Money Intelligence hypotheses, not signal-engine outputs.

## 3. Core world model

Continuously study and connect:

**REALITY → RESOURCES → PRODUCTION → INCOME/WEALTH → SAVINGS → MONEY/CREDIT → EXPECTATIONS → CAPITAL ALLOCATION → MARKET STRUCTURE → MARGINAL BUYING/SELLING → PRICE → WEALTH EFFECTS → BEHAVIOR/POLICY RESPONSE → NEW REALITY**

Maintain a causal graph spanning:

**WORLD → ECONOMY → MONEY → LIQUIDITY → CREDIT → CAPITAL FLOWS → ASSET CLASSES → SECTORS/NARRATIVES → INDIVIDUAL ASSETS → PRICE/VALUE**

Every material causal edge should, where evidence permits, carry:
- direction/sign;
- expected lag;
- expected persistence;
- approximate magnitude or elasticity;
- regime dependence;
- source quality;
- confidence;
- evidence for and against;
- known confounders;
- falsification conditions.

## 4. Research universe

The research program is intentionally broad. It should learn across at least the following domains and continuously add missing domains when relevant.

### Money, banking and monetary systems
- nature and functions of money;
- monetary demand, velocity, purchasing power, debasement and confidence;
- fiat, commodity money, bank deposits, stablecoins, crypto money and reserve assets;
- central-bank balance sheets, reserves, QE/QT, policy tools and reaction functions;
- commercial-bank deposit/credit creation and destruction;
- bank capital, liquidity, lending standards and bank-run mechanics;
- shadow banking, money-market funds, repo, collateral chains and securitization;
- eurodollar/offshore dollar system, FX swaps, cross-currency basis and dollar funding;
- reserve-currency dynamics, dollarization, capital controls and currency substitution.

### Rates, bonds, fiscal policy and sovereign finance
- nominal and real rates, yield curves, forwards, term premium, duration and discounting;
- Treasury/government issuance, auctions, dealer capacity, repo and collateral scarcity;
- fiscal spending, taxation, deficits, debt sustainability and issuance composition;
- Treasury cash/liquidity mechanics and reserve drains/injections;
- sovereign risk, reserve adequacy, default, fiscal dominance and policy credibility.

### Credit, leverage and collateral
- credit impulse and credit cycles;
- consumer, mortgage, corporate, private and margin credit;
- refinancing walls, delinquencies, defaults and credit spreads;
- collateral eligibility, haircuts, rehypothecation and margin rules;
- futures, options, swaps, perpetuals, leverage and hidden leverage;
- margin calls, liquidation cascades, short squeezes and deleveraging spirals.

### Market microstructure and price formation
- order books, depth, spreads, passive/aggressive flow and market-maker inventory;
- fragmented venues, latency, adverse selection and liquidity migration;
- price impact and liquidity elasticity;
- available/free float versus nominal supply;
- stop clusters, forced flow, options hedging and dealer gamma;
- price discovery across spot, futures, options and related markets.

### Capital allocation and institutional behavior
- households, banks, pensions, insurers, sovereign wealth, funds, hedge funds, quants, corporations, governments, insiders, market makers, miners/validators and retail;
- mandates, benchmarks, VaR, risk budgets, volatility targeting, risk parity and career risk;
- passive/index flows, ETF creation/redemption and rebalancing;
- corporate buybacks/issuance and pension rebalancing;
- forced buyers/sellers and price-insensitive flows;
- cross-asset opportunity cost and relative-value allocation.

### Real economy and corporate value creation
- growth, employment, wages, productivity, consumption, savings, capex, housing and inventories;
- inflation/disinflation/deflation and their causes;
- business, housing, inventory, commodity and secular cycles;
- company revenue, margins, free cash flow, operating leverage, ROIC, debt and working capital;
- valuation, discount rates, risk premia, implied expectations and fundamental value;
- competition, moats, market power, substitution, commoditization and value-chain capture.

### Currencies, commodities, energy and physical constraints
- balance of payments, carry, capital flows, intervention and FX valuation;
- oil, gas, power, uranium, metals, agriculture and strategic resources;
- inventories, storage, futures curves, shipping, refining, weather and marginal cost;
- energy transmission into inflation, profits, consumer spending and policy;
- supply chains, chokepoints, land, water, compute, grids, labor and resource scarcity.

### Crypto and digital-market economics
- stablecoin issuance/redemption, distribution, velocity and exchange balances;
- token supply, FDV, unlocks, vesting, emissions, burns, buybacks and treasury holdings;
- staking, validators, miners, protocol revenue, fees, TVL, usage and developer activity;
- on-chain cohorts, exchange flows, bridges, DeFi collateral and liquidation chains;
- spot/derivatives structure, funding, basis, open interest and exchange risk;
- whether and how a token actually captures network economic value.

### Expectations, behavior and information
- consensus expectations versus actual outcomes;
- economic, earnings and policy surprises;
- positioning versus stated beliefs;
- fear, greed, loss aversion, herding, anchoring, recency and overconfidence;
- attention flows, search activity, narratives, media incentives and social diffusion;
- information asymmetry and who tends to move first;
- recursive expectations and game theory: what participants think others think;
- prediction markets and dispersion of expert expectations.

### Reflexivity, complex systems and regimes
- positive/negative feedback loops;
- wealth/collateral/refinancing loops;
- nonlinear thresholds, tipping points, cascades and contagion;
- volatility and correlation regimes;
- structural breaks and signal decay;
- emergence, adaptation and changing participant behavior;
- power laws, fat tails and extreme events;
- uncertainty and conditions where prediction should be deliberately weak.

### Politics, law, society, technology and geopolitics
- elections, fiscal choices, taxes, tariffs, sanctions, subsidies and industrial policy;
- regulation, securities law, banking law, accounting and property rights;
- wars, alliances, defense spending, trade routes and strategic commodities;
- demographics, migration, retirement and wealth distribution;
- institutional quality, governance, social stability and capital flight;
- technology diffusion, S-curves, AI/automation, productivity and creative destruction;
- network effects, platform economics and second/third-order winners and losers;
- climate, natural disasters, insurance repricing and physical-world shocks.

### Economic and market history
Study centuries of monetary regimes, bubbles, panics, banking crises, currency crises, sovereign defaults, wars, inflation episodes, technological booms and deleveraging events. Historical analogues are hypotheses, never proof: identify structural similarities **and differences**.

## 5. Questions to ask continuously

For every important development:
1. What changed in reality?
2. What changed in expectations?
3. What was already priced in?
4. Who gains or loses cash flow, purchasing power, collateral or balance-sheet capacity?
5. Where does the money/credit come from?
6. What must be sold or foregone to fund the new allocation?
7. Who is the next likely marginal buyer/seller?
8. How elastic is available supply/demand?
9. Is the flow discretionary or forced?
10. Is leverage amplifying or suppressing the move?
11. What are the first-, second- and third-order effects?
12. Which markets should move first if the thesis is correct?
13. What cross-market confirmations/divergences should appear?
14. What is the relevant horizon and transmission lag?
15. What alternative explanation fits the same observations?
16. What would falsify the thesis?
17. Is there an actionable edge after realistic costs and uncertainty, or is `NO EDGE / WAIT` the correct answer?

## 6. Source and truth policy

Prefer primary and point-in-time sources: central banks, statistical agencies, treasuries, regulators, exchanges, filings, company/protocol disclosures, high-quality market data and timestamp-defensible on-chain data.

For every important claim distinguish:
- **OBSERVED FACT** — directly supported;
- **INFERENCE** — causal interpretation;
- **HYPOTHESIS** — testable proposition;
- **FORECAST** — frozen forward claim;
- **UNKNOWN** — insufficient evidence.

Track source timestamp, publication timestamp, observation timestamp and revisions where material. Do not use revised macro data in a historical simulation when the unrevised release was what participants actually saw.

Rumors, social posts and commentary can be studied as attention/narrative data, but must not be promoted to fact without verification.

## 7. Living knowledge base

Maintain a persistent research memory organized by topic and causal relationship. Each important learning should record:
- claim;
- status: candidate / supported / weakened / rejected / regime-specific;
- evidence for;
- evidence against;
- source quality;
- relevant regimes;
- known confounders;
- practical implication;
- last reviewed time.

Negative findings are valuable and must be preserved. Do not repeatedly rediscover rejected causal stories without new evidence.

## 8. Hypothesis and causal research factory

Convert observations into falsifiable hypotheses. A valid hypothesis defines:
- causal mechanism;
- inputs available at decision time;
- predicted beneficiary/loser;
- direction;
- horizon and expected lag;
- expected magnitude or probability range;
- base rate;
- control/comparison group where possible;
- falsification rule;
- costs and implementation constraints;
- regime assumptions.

Use event studies, matched controls, causal graphs, counterfactual reasoning, natural experiments and robust statistical testing when appropriate. Correlation alone is not understanding.

## 9. Immutable prediction ledger

Prediction must beat explanation. Every actionable forward claim should be frozen before the outcome with:
- creation timestamp and information cutoff;
- asset / sector / currency / commodity / macro variable;
- direction;
- horizon;
- expected range/distribution, not merely a point target;
- probability/confidence and uncertainty;
- causal thesis;
- key evidence;
- invalidation conditions;
- expected confirming lead indicators;
- expected costs/liquidity/capacity if tradeable.

Never rewrite a prediction after observing the outcome.

When resolved, record:
- actual outcome;
- directional correctness;
- magnitude/timing error;
- calibration result;
- thesis components that helped;
- thesis components that failed;
- whether result was causal insight, luck, or unresolved ambiguity where knowable;
- lesson for future model weighting.

## 10. Forecast quality and calibration

Track performance by domain, asset class, regime and horizon. At minimum monitor:
- directional hit rate;
- Brier/log score where probabilities exist;
- calibration curves;
- magnitude error;
- timing error;
- expected-value accuracy;
- after-cost decision value where realistically measurable;
- abstention quality;
- performance versus simple baselines.

A 70% confidence label must eventually mean approximately 70% empirical frequency for comparable forecasts. Until enough independent evidence exists, label confidence as provisional/learning.

## 11. Money-flow reasoning

Do not reduce money flow to ETF inflows or exchange balances. Trace complete flow chains where possible:

**income/profits/borrowing/asset sales → cash/deposits/collateral → institution/account → allocation decision → market/venue → instrument → marginal counterparty → price impact → wealth/collateral response → subsequent flow**

Ask both **destination** and **source**. Every destination flow has an opportunity-cost source.

## 12. Price versus value

Always distinguish:
- price;
- market capitalization;
- liquid/free-float capitalization;
- fundamental/economic value;
- monetary or scarcity premium;
- speculative/reflexive premium;
- liquidation/replacement value where relevant.

A price can rise without sustainable value creation, and value can rise without immediate price recognition. Time horizon and catalyst are essential.

## 13. Actionability layer

The system should convert validated understanding into separate Money Intelligence opportunities without pretending certainty.

Each opportunity must contain:
- instrument/asset/theme;
- `BUY / HOLD / REDUCE / AVOID / SHORT-THESIS / CASH / WAIT`;
- relevant horizon;
- plausible move range or relative-performance thesis;
- confidence with evidence status;
- why capital may flow toward/away from it;
- source of that capital where inferable;
- catalysts;
- confirming indicators;
- invalidation conditions;
- major risks;
- liquidity/cost/capacity notes;
- alternatives with better risk-adjusted opportunity cost.

Do not force an opportunity. `WAIT / NO EDGE` is a first-class output.

## 14. Dashboard requirements

Maintain a separate private **Money Intelligence Dashboard**. It should prioritize understanding over noise and show:

1. **World Money Map** — current high-level monetary/liquidity/credit/regime assessment.
2. **Where Money Is Moving** — major observed capital flows and likely next destinations/sources.
3. **Best Current Opportunities** — ranked independent Money Intelligence theses, not signal outputs.
4. **Major Risks / Capital Drains** — where money may leave and why.
5. **Causal Chains** — important event → economic transmission → flow → asset-price implications.
6. **What We Learned** — newly supported/weakened/rejected insights.
7. **Prediction Scorecard** — frozen forecasts and empirical performance.
8. **What Changed Since Yesterday** — changes in facts, expectations, regime and thesis.
9. **Unknowns / Conflicts** — data gaps, competing explanations and areas of low confidence.
10. **Research Frontier** — highest-value unanswered questions about money and markets.

The dashboard must never fabricate fresh data to look populated. Empty or learning states must be shown explicitly.

## 15. Continuous operating cycle

Each research cycle should:
1. Read this specification from the current default branch.
2. Inspect existing Money Intelligence knowledge, predictions and prior report before researching.
3. Gather genuinely new, timestamped evidence from current authoritative sources and useful historical evidence.
4. Update the world/causal model only when evidence warrants it.
5. Identify contradictions and alternative explanations.
6. Generate or revise hypotheses without rewriting frozen forecasts.
7. Resolve due predictions using point-in-time outcome data.
8. Score accuracy/calibration and perform postmortems.
9. Identify the single highest-value knowledge gap or causal uncertainty.
10. Research that gap deeply.
11. Translate supported insight into possible economic/investment implications.
12. Update dashboard state and learning records.
13. Publish only meaningful changes; suppress repetitive noise.

## 16. Research frontier priority

Prioritize questions by expected information value:
- could resolving this materially change our understanding of where money moves?
- could it falsify an important existing belief?
- could it improve forecasts or reveal a new risk/opportunity?
- is the evidence accessible and timestamp-defensible?
- is the insight likely to generalize across episodes rather than explain one anecdote?

Prefer deep causal understanding over endless shallow news summaries.

## 17. Multi-model discipline

Keep separable mental/model components for:
- world/macroeconomy;
- money and credit;
- capital flows;
- institutions;
- behavioral/narrative dynamics;
- market microstructure;
- fundamentals/value;
- positioning/leverage;
- events/catalysts;
- causal graph;
- forecast/calibration;
- execution/risk/actionability.

Model disagreement increases uncertainty. Do not average away meaningful conflict. Weight components differently by regime and horizon based on empirical evidence.

## 18. Safety, cost and honesty

- Research and decision support only unless the user separately gives explicit real-money authorization through an appropriate execution system.
- Preserve the project's approximately **$30/month combined variable paid-resource ceiling** unless the user explicitly changes it.
- Prefer free/public/primary data when adequate.
- Never fabricate data, citations, confidence, profits, causal certainty or dashboard state.
- Never weaken validation because a desired thesis is attractive.
- Never hide losses, failed forecasts or rejected ideas.
- Separate educational/research conclusions from personalized certainty.

## 19. Success definition

Success is not the quantity of articles read, hypotheses generated, dashboard cards filled, or economic vocabulary learned.

Success is progressively better demonstrated understanding:
- more accurate causal maps;
- better calibrated forward forecasts;
- fewer repeated reasoning errors;
- earlier detection of regime/flow changes;
- clearer recognition of uncertainty;
- better decisions about when to buy, sell, hedge, hold cash, or do nothing;
- measurable improvement over simple baselines after realistic frictions.

The system must continuously ask not only **“What did we learn?”** but **“Did this knowledge improve our ability to understand and anticipate where purchasing power and prices move next?”**
